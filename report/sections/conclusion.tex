The answer to the title question is no, for the books as pre-registered. Classic price and volume signals do predict the cross-section of NSE

(net IR \R{p1.oos.ir_net}, interval [\R{p1.oos.ir_net.ci95_lo}, \R{p1.oos.ir_net.ci95_hi}]), and the F\&O long--short book loses more. The deflated
Sharpe is \R{p1.dev.dsr}. This is a null result for net alpha, and it is reported as one.

The failure is specific: the score's predictive power is slow (its IC does not decay over 21 sessions), but the weekly re-ranking trades it
\R{p1.oos.turnover_oneway_pa} times a year, and spreads plus STT eat the edge. The natural next experiment is a slower book (monthly rebalance,
wider hold buffer, or a turnover penalty in the optimizer), pre-registered against a new holdout, since this one has been spent.
