\begin{abstract}
We ask whether eight classic daily price and volume signals predict cross-sectional returns on the National Stock Exchange of India after
realistic costs, using a survivorship-free point-in-time panel rebuilt from exchange archives, a pre-registered protocol, a trial ledger and a
locked out-of-sample window read once. The signals work: a LightGBM combination selected by a pre-registered rule has a walk-forward rank IC
of \R{combo.headline.rank_ic} and a holdout rank IC of \R{oos.score.mean_rank_ic_h5} ($t=$ \R{oos.score.t_nw_h5}). The portfolios do not survive costs.
A weekly long-only top-decile book beats the Nifty 500 price index gross (development IR \R{p1.dev.ir_gross}) but, trading
\R{p1.dev.turnover_oneway_pa} times a year, loses to it net: development IR \R{p1.dev.ir_net}, deflated Sharpe \R{p1.dev.dsr} over
\R{honesty.n_all} logged trials; holdout (2024-11-01 to 2026-10-30) IR \R{p1.oos.ir_net}, 95\% bootstrap interval
[\R{p1.oos.ir_net.ci95_lo}, \R{p1.oos.ir_net.ci95_hi}]. An F\&O long--short book has a holdout net Sharpe of \R{p2.oos.sharpe_net}.
Spreads and STT account for most of the cost drag. Every number in this paper is rendered from the run files by \texttt{qr ledger}.
\end{abstract}
